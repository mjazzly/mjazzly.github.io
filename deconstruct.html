DECONSTRUCT-01 · A number on a tile

As published. What it rests on, a layer at a time:

  1. 1 back

    The count

    What one unit on the tile is: a record in the export, or a person.

    The same person entered twice is two records. Four people in these rows appear twice, each pair sharing one email.

    The choices: Records (as published), People.

  2. 2 back

    The word “qualified”

    Which contacts the word covers: anyone who ever reached the stage, or only those still in it.

    Contacts move on after they qualify, to an opportunity, to a customer, or out. Counting only the ones still in the stage drops them.

    The choices: Reached it at any time (as published), In the stage now.

  3. 3 back

    The date

    Which date puts a contact inside July to September.

    A contact created in September and qualified in October falls in one quarter by one date and in the next by the other.

    The choices: Date qualified (as published), Date created.

An on-time rate

DECONSTRUCT-02 · An on-time rate
On time
79%
19 of 24 flights, June

As published. What it rests on, a layer at a time:

  1. 1 back

    The rule

    How late a flight can be and still count as on time.

    One public reporting body counts a flight as on time when it arrives less than 15 minutes after schedule, so at 15 minutes it is late. A stricter or looser line moves flights across it.

    The choices: Under 15 minutes late (as published), Not late at all, Under 30 minutes late.

    Source: Bureau of Transportation Statistics, how flight delays are calculated

  2. 2 back

    The flights that never arrived

    What happens to the two cancelled flights and the one diverted flight.

    Counted, they stay in the total as not on time. Left out, the total shrinks and the rate rises.

    The choices: Counted as not on time (as published), Left out.

  3. 3 back

    Where it is measured

    Which moment is timed: arrival at the gate, or departure from it.

    Flights make up time in the air and lose it on the ground, so a late departure can still arrive on time, and the reverse.

    The choices: At arrival (as published), At departure.

    The rows

    The 24 flights in June, as scheduled and as flown. The ones counted on time are lit.

As published. What it rests on, a layer at a time:

  1. 1 back

    The gate

    The checker that compares every row with the answer written before the first run, and the rule the row was made under: an owner only when the transcript names the person in the same sentence, and “not stated” for every blank.

    The rule decides what goes in the owner cell; the checker shows whether the row kept to it.

    Matched The answer written before the first run: owner Mei Ling Chong, due 2026-10-24, and the close by transcript 05 checked too.

    The run’s own note, transcript 05: “Line 1 ("the scenario B model is updated and with Hannah") read as a closure of A005.”

  2. 2 back

    The sentence

    How the line in the transcript names, or does not name, who will do the work. Three lines from the same meeting, each phrased one way.

    A name in the same sentence makes an owner. A name one line later, or no name at all, leaves the owner “not stated”, and the row is still kept, because an unowned action is the one most worth chasing.

    The choices: Name in the same sentence (as published), Name in the next sentence, No name.

  3. 3 back · the floor

    The transcript

    The meeting as it was recorded, line by line. The line the row was read from is lit.

    The row can only say what these lines say.

    Line 5 lit: the line the row was read from. The line that closed it is lit below.

The model reads the transcript; the rule and the checker decide what it may write. A row with “not stated” in it is not a failure: it is the row telling you who to ask.

The words on this page

Every term used on this page, in plain words.

  • Output. The finished result a reader sees first: a number on a tile, a rate, a row on a tracker.
  • Layer. One choice or one source an output rests on, one step behind the one above it.
  • Step back. Show the next layer under the output. Step forward hides it again; a choice made on it is kept.
  • As published. The setting an output was first shown with. Each layer marks it, and Reset returns every layer to it.
  • Floor. The last layer: the rows or the record, with nothing left to choose.
  • Export. The rows a system writes out, as they came, before anyone counts them.
  • Record. One row in an export: here, one contact.
  • Duplicate. The same person held in two records. Here the two share one email.
  • Stage. Where a contact stands: new, contacted, qualified, opportunity, customer or disqualified.
  • Qualified. A contact judged worth a sales conversation. The stage is reached on one date; the contact may move on after it.
  • Quarter. Three months counted together; here July to September.
  • On time. By the published rule, a flight that arrives at its gate less than 15 minutes after its scheduled time.
  • Cancelled. A flight that never left.
  • Diverted. A flight that left but landed somewhere other than where it was going.
  • Tracker. A running list of actions from meetings, each with an owner, a due date and a status, added to and never rewritten.
  • Owner. The person a row says will do the work, written only when the transcript names them in the same sentence as the commitment.
  • Not stated. What goes in any cell the transcript does not fill. A blank is a blank, not a guess.
  • Transcript. The meeting as it was recorded, line by line, each line numbered.
  • Checker. Code that compares each row with the answer written before the first run and records whether it matched.
  • Answer written before the first run. The expected rows for each test case, written by a person before the assistant ever ran, so a run cannot shape its own test.
  • Run. One full pass of the tracker over every transcript, with its own id and record.
  • Blind. A run made with the answers out of its reach.

Every result on this page is a recount of rows you can open, or a row read from a named record.
