Mental Models · How I Reason

The models I build by

Good work runs on a small set of reusable models. These are the five I use, and they work as one cycle: build a system, refine it, absorb its misses, and make it ready for the cases nobody saw coming. The fifth grows the person doing the work.

Maker / Marker / Multiplier builds it, Iteration Loops refines it, Productive Failure absorbs the misses, Introduce Chaos hardens it, and the Founders Triangle grows the person underneath all of it.

Maker / Marker / Multiplier

Maker: build the process end to end. Marker: once the process is clear, the job becomes checking each checkpoint, human or AI, against the standard. Multiplier: a proven process reveals where to improve and scale the whole workflow.

In practiceIt is the arc of every system I ship: build it, make it checkable, then scale what works.

Iteration Loops

Run every piece of work end to end, then review it honestly: what worked, what did not, what to improve. The output of one loop is the input of the next.

In practiceNo deliverable is final, it is the current best version, and the loop never fully closes.

Productive Failure

Fail fast, refocus faster. A miss is information, not identity. The discipline, associated with Federer's next-point mindset, is to let the last point go and give the next one your full attention.

In practiceSpeed of recovery beats fear of failure, so experiments stay cheap and frequent.

Introduce Chaos To Your System

Deliberately imagine the edge cases and worst-case scenarios that could break a system, workflow, or framework, then design for them. The goal is antifragility: even the cases you did not foresee land inside a blanket you already built.

In practiceI stress-test a process by attacking it before a client or a deadline does.

Founders Triangle

Three sides to hone deliberately: Depth, real skill in a craft. Domain, knowing the field it applies to. Distribution, the ability to communicate and reach an audience. Strength in all three compounds, and a gap in any one caps the other two.

In practiceThe reason I pair building systems with documenting and explaining them clearly, capability is only worth what you can transfer.
