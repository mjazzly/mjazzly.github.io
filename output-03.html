The metricsmetrics.json

  • ordersCount of orders placed in the period, returned or not.
  • revenueSum of the order value of every order placed in the period, before returns.
  • returnsCount of orders placed in the period that were later returned.
  • returns_rateReturns divided by orders, as a percent: the share of orders placed in the period that were later returned.
  • returned_valueSum of the order value of the orders placed in the period that were later returned.
  • net_revenueRevenue minus returned value: the order value of the orders placed in the period that were not returned.
  • average_order_valueRevenue divided by orders: the mean order value of the orders placed in the period.
  • largest_orderThe highest single order value among the orders placed in the period.
  • mom_growthRevenue in the month minus revenue in the month before, divided by the month before, as a percent. The period must be one month, and January has no month before it in the data.
  • qoq_growthRevenue in the quarter minus revenue in the quarter before, divided by the quarter before, as a percent. The period must be one quarter, and Q1 has no quarter before it in the data.
  • revenue_shareA group's revenue divided by the revenue of every group in the period, as a percent. One dimension is required: the groups are its values.
  • orders_shareA group's orders divided by the orders of every group in the period, as a percent. One dimension is required: the groups are its values.

The words on this page

  • Metric. A measure defined once in code with its own definition, such as revenue or returns rate. The model picks a metric; it never writes the query itself.
  • Definition. What the metric counts, in plain words, written before the first run and returned with every answer.
  • Period. The stretch of time the answer covers: a year, a month, a quarter, a half or a range of months.
  • Filter. A limit on which orders count, such as one channel. Only values that exist in the data are accepted.
  • Computed over. How many order rows the figure was worked out from, out of every row loaded, and which version of the data was loaded.
  • Lineage. How the answer relates to earlier ones: the first answer to its question, the same as an earlier answer, or changed because the data changed.
  • Refusal. A question the code will not answer: a metric it does not define, a column the table does not have, a named person, or an estimate. It names the nearest question it can answer.
  • Run. One pass over the question set by one model, recorded with its id, its tokens and its cost.
